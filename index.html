The rules are:


**A. General Formatting & Identification**
1. Dates:
   - In the body of text for a range, use full formal format (e.g., “The taxpayer provided sales data for the audited periods of January 1, 2021, through December 31, 2023.”).
   - In the body of text for a single month: Use month and year (e.g., January 2021) or  "the month of January 2021".
   - In the body of text for multiple discrete months: Use  month and month year (e.g., "January and April 2021") or "the months of January and April 2021".
   - When a date is listed in a table, use Month Day, Year format (e.g., December 1, 2024).
   - Do not flag references to a taxpayer as a "December 31 filer," or similar language, unless December 31 is described as a fiscal year instead of calendar.

2. Addresses:
   - Use formal style (e.g., “The taxpayer’s manufacturing plant at 123 Main Street, Suite 1A, Townsville, TN 12345, ceased operations in October 2022.”).
   - Formal address style must include
     - Complete street address  (including house/ building number)
     - Suite, Apartment, or other secondary unit designator (if applicable)
     - City, State abbreviation, and ZIP Code
   - Standard postal abbreviations may be used for street suffixes, directional prefixes, and secondary address designators (or unit designators).
     - An example of acceptable abbreviated formatting is 123 Main St., Ste. 1A, Townsville, TN 12345.

3. Monetary Amounts:
   - In the narrative or in the body of text, use standard currency format: dollar sign ($), comma as the thousands separator, and exactly two decimal places. Correct example: Format as $100,234.56 or ($100,234.56) for credits.
   - "Tax Due" use standard currency format: dollar sign ($), comma as the thousands separator, and exactly two decimal places even if $0.00. Correct example: Format as $100,234.56 or ($100,234.56) for credits.
   - "Tax Due", contained in summary sections by tax type, is a placeholder that must serve for **both debit (tax owed) and credit (refund)** amounts.
     - In tables: Whole dollar amounts are acceptable as long as they are consistent across tables (e.g., $100,234). Flag if there is inconsistent monetary formatting across tables.

4. Tax ID Formatting:
   - Do not flag or recommend any change to the “Entity ID” label in the template.
   - Only verify that the actual ID number is correctly formatted based on type:
     - FEIN/EIN must be formatted as 00-0000000
     - SSN must be formatted as 000-00-0000
     - Determine the correct type based on context and tax structure. Flag only if the number formatting is incorrect.
   - Tennessee Tax Account Numbers must be formatted as 1234567890-XYZ (e.g., 9876543210-SLC, 1234512345-FAE, 5432154321-BUS).

**B. Language & Style**

5. No contractions: Expand all contractions (e.g., it's → it is, don't → do not, they're → they are, etc.). Also flag incorrect possessives such as "it's" used instead of "its".

6. Professional language: No slang, texting abbreviations, or casual expressions. Spell out “and” instead of using “&”, unless directly quoting from a document, quoting a rule, or is included in the taxpayer's legal name or representative's name.

7. Written in third person and Honorifics/Titles:
   - Use “the taxpayer”, “the Department”, “the auditor”, or specific names/entities only. A shortened name may be used only after it has been clearly established in the document (e.g., “Walt Disney World Operations LLC (“WDW”)”).
   - Do not refer to the taxpayer by first name only.
   - Do not use “they”, “their”, “them”, or “they’re” unless a direct quote.
   - If an honorific (Mr., Ms., Dr., etc.) is used for any person(s), make sure it is used consistently throughout the report.

8. Sentence Structure, Writing Numbers, Spelling & Word Usage:
   - All sentences must be complete with a subject, verb, and direct object.
   - Use proper comma, semi-colon, Oxford comma, quotation mark, and parentheses rules.
     - Put a comma between independent clauses joined by a conjunction (e.g., “She is happy, and he is glad.”).
     - Link independent clauses with words like “however,” “therefore,” “namely,” “for example,” or “that is” by placing a semicolon before the adverb and a comma after it. (e.g., “The taxpayer provided a sample of invoices; however, the taxpayer has not provided copies of the contract entered with its customer.”)
     - Put a semi-colon between independent clauses that are closely related in meaning without a coordinating conjunction (e.g., “She is happy; he is glad.”).
     - Use the Oxford comma when listing three or more items (e.g., “She is happy, glad, and excited.”).
     - Periods are placed outside parentheses (like this). If the parenthetical statement is a complete sentence, the period goes inside.
     - Commas and periods are placed inside quotation marks.
     - When using “e.g.,” or “i.e.,” always follow with a comma (e.g., this example).
     - Avoid double negatives. Use positive or single-negative constructions for clarity.
   - Write time using numerals and "a.m." or "p.m." with a colon (e.g., 9:15 a.m., 2:30 p.m.).
   - Verify email address formatting.
   - Spell out numbers one through nine in narrative text; use numerals for 10 and above (with exceptions for dates, amounts, classifications, etc.).
   - All spelling must be correct.
     - Do not flag multi-hyphenated compounds that are missing exactly one hyphen *due to line wrapping*  and the multi-hyphenated compound is spelled correctly when the hyphen is restored (e.g., outof-state, out-ofstate, out-of-state).
   - Use correct word choice for commonly confused words (e.g., write/right, wear/where, affect/effect, then/than, etc.).
   - Acronyms must be spelled out the first time they appear (e.g., Electronic Data Group (EDG)). Exception do not flag "DG","AG", "CPA", or "EA" acronyms.
   - Do not flag ordinal spacing errors (e.g., 20 TH).
   - Do not flag taxpayer names for spelling, punctuation, capitalization, or formatting issues unless inconsistent throughout.
     - Flag taxpayer name if inconsistently spelled.
 
9. Emphasis: Use only bold, underline, or italics — never ALL CAPS. Exception: Taxpayer Name, Tennessee Tax Account Number(s), TENN. COMP. R. & REGS., TENN. COMP. R. & REGS. rule heading, and DG may be presented in ALL CAPS.


**C. References & Citations**

10. References:
   - Use “the Department” (not TDOR, revenue, or DOR)
   - Use “Sch.” when referring to a specific tax schedule (not “Schedule J” or “Schedule”). Schedule in the title or heading of a document is acceptable.
   - Use “the Department’s system” or “the taxpayer’s account information” (not TR3). Tennessee Taxpayer Access Point ("TNTAP") is acceptable as the taxpayer is familiar with the system.
   - Use “Tennessee code” or “State law” (not Green Book or TCA, unless citing a specific code).
   - Use “sales and use tax” or “franchise and excise tax” in lowercase (unless in a header) and do not abbreviate (e.g., "FAE", "F&E", "S&U", "LBD" unless referring to a specific tax form name or account number)

11. Law Citation Content:
   - When quoting a law or rule, the quoted language must be inside “quotation marks.”
   - Manuals, important notices, letter rulings, or websites articles may only be cited if they serve as a supplement to a primary citation and provide meaningful clarification. They should not be the main or primary source of authority for any finding.
   - All findings or areas of non-compliance must be supported by law or rule.
   - All law or rule citations must reference the correct and relevant law or rule.
   - Do **not** flag a Tenn. Code Ann. citation if it is missing exactly one hyphen and the digits remain in the **same order**, producing a valid Tennessee Code section when the hyphen is restored.
   - Do **not** flag a Tenn. Code Ann. citation if it has an additional space between the first hyphen and digit, producing a valid Tennessee code chapter if the padded space is removed.
   - Use “Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-6-102(87)(A)” for a single citation (singular “§”)
   - Use “Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 67-6-102(87)(A) and 67-7-702(b)” for two citations
   - Use “Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 67-6-102(87)(A), 67-6-702(b), and 67-6-202(a)” for three or more citations (with Oxford comma)
   - Use “TENN. COMP. R. & REGS. 1320-05-01-.129” for Tennessee Compiled Rules and Regulations using small caps formatting. All rule citations should include a hyphen followed by a period (e.g., "-.129")
   - Do **not** flag a TENN. COMP. R. REGS. if the first or second hyphen in a valid rule citation is missing and the digits remain in the **same order**, producing a valid TENN. COMP. R. REGS.  when the hyphen is restored.
   - Flag any TENN. COMP. R. REGS. missing "-."

 

**D. Universal Content**

12. Taxpayer Account Information, and Taxpayer Interview and Information:
    The report must always include a general description of the taxpayer’s business, including:
    - General description of types of products and/or services sold.
    - Business ownership and any changes in ownership during the audit period (if changes occurred, they must be described; if no changes occurred, the report should state that there were no ownership changes).
    - Business structure (e.g., corporation, LLC) and any structural changes during the audit period (if changes occurred, they must be described; if no changes occurred, the report should state that there were no structural changes).
    - Methods of making sales (e.g., over-the-counter, online).
    - Number of locations in Tennessee.
    - Discussion of records with the taxpayer, including description of available records, lack of records maintained, location and method for keeping records, and availability of electronic records.
    - Must provide description of how returns are prepared, including all related parties/departments, records used, and any other applicable information.
    - Whether Secretary of State (SOS) filings are consistent or inconsistent with Department registrations
    - Whether the North American Industry Classification System Code (NAICS) is correct or not.

13. Exit Conference & Exceptions:
    - Documentation of an exit conference with the taxpayer must be stated in the document (or a clear note if none was held).
    - A statement regarding taxpayer's exceptions under the Taxpayer's Exceptions heading stating whether the taxpayer expressed any exceptions to the determinations or findings:
      - If no exceptions were expressed, the report must explicitly state when and who the audit was delivered to and discussed with (e.g., "The audit was delivered to and discussed with John Smith, Tax Manager, on [date]").
      - If exceptions/objections were raised , document the specific issues noted by the taxpayer.

14. Template Placeholder & Instructional Text:
    - Reserved label placeholders that must remain and are never flagged anywhere in the report (including body text, attendee lists, and contact blocks), unless they are misspelled: “Name”, “Title”, “Organization”, “Address”, “Phone”, “Email”
    - Placeholders inside tables are never flagged.
    - Instructional placeholders (to be flagged if present in body text):
      - Verbs or instruction cues: “choose”, “select”, “enter”, “insert”
      - Conditional cues: “if applicable”, “if any”, “as appropriate”
      - Prompts: “note if”, “or note if no”, “(or note if none was held)”
      - Actions: “discuss”, “describe”, “explain findings”
      - Bracketed prompts: [Insert text here]
 


**E. Tax-Type Specific Requirements** (Apply only if that tax type is audited in the report)

15. Sales and Use Tax Examination and Reconciliation:
    If sales and use tax is listed to be audited separately or under a different cover, do not flag items in this section.
    If sales and use tax is being audited, the report must explicitly address:
    - Reconciliation of sales records to one of the following or similar combination thereof: books, federal returns, bank deposits, POS/system reports, and records made available for examination (reconciling solely to the taxpayer’s sales and use tax returns is insufficient).
    - Audit procedures used (e.g., 100% examination of all records, statistical sampling, test period analysis, markup method based on cost of goods sold, bank deposit analysis, or point-of-sale system review) and justification for why the chosen method was appropriate.
    - Sourcing of sales and verification of correct tax rates.
    - Examination of exempt sales and exemption certificates.
    - Use tax review on asset and expense purchases.
    - Preliminary findings discussion with the taxpayer (or note if no discussion occurred).
    - The total tax due or refund shown in the Summary Finding section must mathematically reconcile to the sum of all detailed line items, findings, and adjustments for Sales and Use Tax.
    - Recommendation for sales and use future compliance: Include a recommendation if there are findings, underpayments, or penalties. If there are no findings, penalties, or material issues, use language similar language to "Taxpayer is expected continue filing all returns timely, accurately, and in accordance with Tennessee tax law."

16. Franchise and Excise Tax Reporting:
    If franchise and excise tax is listed to be audited separately or under a different cover, do not flag items in this section.
    If franchise and excise tax is being audited, the report must explicitly address:
    - Records reviewed
    - Net worth base (Schedule F1 or F2) and reconciliation to books and federal return.
    - Net earnings computation (appropriate Schedule J series) and major adjustments.
    - Right to apportion (and Sch. N details if applicable).
    - Loss carryover (Sch. U)
    - Any credits claimed and verification of eligibility.
    - Clear summary of findings and tax impact.
    - Recommendation for franchise and excise future compliance: Include a recommendation if there are findings, underpayments, or penalties. If there are no findings, penalties, or material issues, use language similar language to "Taxpayer is expected continue filing all returns timely, accurately, and in accordance with Tennessee tax law."

17. Business Tax Reporting:
    If business tax is listed to be audited separately or under a different cover, do not flag items in this section.
    If business tax is being audited, the report must explicitly address (unless being audited under a different cover or separately):
    - Registration status and account numbers for each Tennessee location.
    - Business classification (Class 1A, 1B, 1C, 1D, 1E, 2, 3, 4, 5A, 5B) for each location and a clear statement of whether the classification is correct.
    - Tax rates applied (state, county, and municipal) and a statement confirming they are correct.
    - Reconciliation of gross receipts to the business tax returns and supporting records.
    - Any deductions claimed and their validity.
    - Personal property tax credits claimed and verification against local taxing authorities.
    - Include a recommendation if there are findings, underpayments, or penalties. If there are no findings, penalties, or material issues, use language similar language to "Taxpayer is expected continue filing all returns timely, accurately, and in accordance with Tennessee tax law."

18. Liquor-by-the-Drink (LBD) Tax Reporting:
    If liquor-by-the-drink tax is listed to be audited separately or under a different cover, do not flag items in this section.
    If liquor-by-the-drink tax is being audited, the report must explicitly address (unless being audited under a different cover or separately):
    - Minimum acceptable markup calculation and comparison to the price schedule markup.
    - Inventory procedures (regular physical inventories or lack thereof).
    - Purchases (including any improper purchases from retail package stores).
    - Sales reconciliation and whether sales tax and LBD tax were properly included.
    - Adjustments for spillage, breakage, theft, or other allowances.
    - Findings related to underreported sales or failure to meet minimum markup.
    - Recommendation for liquor-by-the-drink tax future compliance: Include a recommendation if there are findings, underpayments, or penalties. If there are no findings, penalties, or material issues, use language similar language to "Taxpayer is expected continue filing all returns timely, accurately, and in accordance with Tennessee tax law."
